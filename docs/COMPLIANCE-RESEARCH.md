# Compliance research notes (2026-10-31)

Verdicts on the rules added for apps built with AI coding agents, with sources. **This is research, not legal advice.** Sources are marked: *primary* (law text or regulator), *secondary* (law-firm or vendor article, not checked against the original). Anything marked **unverified** must be checked with counsel before you rely on it.

## Verdicts on the claims

| # | Claim | Verdict | Notes and sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Row-level security: only the creator and people given access may see data | **Confirmed, top risk** | Missing RLS policies exposed tables in 170 of 1,645 sampled Lovable apps (CVE-2025-48757); vendors say the customer must configure RLS ([Superblocks](https://www.superblocks.com/blog/lovable-vulnerabilities), secondary). The widely repeated "91.5% of vibe-coded apps have a critical flaw" figure comes from a repost; **unverified**. |
| 2 | Never publish keys in the frontend | **Confirmed** | Standard practice; only keys designed to be public (publishable / client tokens) may reach the client. "1 in 4 apps has a key in client JavaScript" is a secondary claim, **unverified**. |
| 3 | Do not use users' uploads for public templates or guidelines; no access when logged out | **Sound policy rule** | No single statute; it follows from purpose limitation (GDPR Art. 5), copyright and personality rights, and from the consent we promise. Added as a published promise and a claim. |
| 4 | Anonymous accounts need a legal-age confirmation | **Right idea, with limits** | A neutral age screen (no date of birth, not leading) is accepted practice for general-audience services. COPPA applies to actual knowledge of under-13 users or child-directed services; amended Rule effective 23 June 2025, full compliance 22 April 2026 ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule), primary). **A tick box is not enough** where the law requires real age assurance (adult content, gambling, some social features; UK Online Safety Act, many US states): self-declaration is not accepted there ([overview](https://didit.me/blog/age-verification-laws-2026/), secondary; state-law status changes often and conflicts between sources: verify). Age levels 13/16 come from GDPR Art. 8 (national choice), 18/21 from content rules such as gambling. |
| 5 | Google Fonts send IP addresses; consent needed in Germany | **Confirmed** | LG München I, 20 Jan 2022, 3 O 17493/20: dynamic loading without consent violated GDPR, EUR 100 damages plus injunction. A later ruling (4 O 13063/22, 30 Mar 2023) refused claims built on crawler-triggered warning letters ([summary](https://www.ihk.de/bergische/recht-und-steuern/wettbewerbsrecht/google-fonts-5646176), secondary; judgment text not retrieved). **Better fix: self-host fonts.** The same applies to CDNs, Gravatar, maps, video and captcha. |
| 6 | Users accept accountability for uploaded images' copyright | **Standard, with limits** | Normal UGC term. It cannot waive mandatory consumer law or our own duties; the text says so. |
| 7 | Turn off session replay | **Sound** | CIPA section 631 wiretap claims over Hotjar/FullStory-type tools continue with mixed results. SB 690 (signed 30 Sep 2026) ends private claims under the **pen-register** theory (section 638.51), not the wiretap theory. A privacy policy alone is generally not treated as consent (practitioner view). Case-level facts **unverified** ([K&L Gates](https://www.klgates.com/thought-leadership/California-Narrows-CIPA-Litigation-SB-690-Ends-Pen-Register-and-Trap-and-Trace-Claims-10-8-2026), [tracker](https://consentpixel.com/blogs/cipa-lawsuit-tracker/), secondary). |
| 8 | Unsubscribe in all emails, with confirmation by magic link and a blocklist | **Needs a split** | FTC (primary): marketing opt-out may not require a fee, **information beyond the email address, or any step other than a reply or visiting a single web page**; honour within 10 business days; the mechanism works 30 days after sending; transactional messages in the five narrow categories may omit it ([FTC guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)). Gmail/Yahoo bulk senders: one-click `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058), honour within 2 days, complaints under 0.3% ([Bird](https://bird.com/docs/knowledge-base/deliverability/gmail-yahoo-requirements), secondary; Microsoft's rules **unverified**). **So:** optional email stops with one click, immediately. The identity-confirmed flow is for "block **all** email" (which also blocks sign-in links), so strangers cannot lock someone out. Sign-in and security emails get no one-click unsubscribe. |
| 9 | Renewal terms next to the pay button | **Confirmed** | Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code section 17602 ([text](https://california.public.law/codes/business_and_professions_code_section_17602), primary): terms must be clear and conspicuous before the agreement is fulfilled and **in visual proximity** to the request for consent (a)(1); affirmative consent before charging (a)(2); retained acknowledgement (a)(3); consent records kept at least 3 years or 1 year after termination (a)(6); reminder 15 to 45 days before renewal for terms of a year or longer (b)(2); online cancel exclusively online with a direct link or button (d)(1); annual reminder for annual plans (h). The FTC click-to-cancel rule was vacated by the Eighth Circuit on 8 July 2025; state laws remain ([Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/eighth-circuit-vacates-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule)). 2026 FTC activity **unverified**. |
| 10 | Consent checklist after magic-link sign-in, remembered in JSON, re-asked on new versions | **Good design** | GDPR requires consent to be demonstrable (Art. 7(1)), granular and unticked (CJEU Planet49, 2019: from my own knowledge, not re-verified here). Store key, version, grant, time, language. Required consents block use; optional ones are asked once per version and may be declined. |
| 11 | Payment provider legal parts in our pages | **Confirmed for Paddle** | Paddle (merchant of record) expects terms of sale, a refund policy (own policy: 14 to 90 day window) and a privacy notice, an explicit MoR disclosure, public pages without login, and acceptance before payment ([Paddle docs](https://developer.paddle.com/partners/embed-billing/seller-go-live/minimum-seller-terms), secondary summary). We cannot disclaim duties the law puts on us. |

## DMCA designated agent: do we register?

**Yes, for every legal entity whose apps store content users upload or post.** Section 512(c)(2) conditions the US "safe harbour" from copyright damages for user-stored content on having a designated agent registered with the US Copyright Office. It is not a criminal or administrative duty (nothing fines you for not registering), but without it you cannot use that safe harbour. It does not replace the takedown process, the repeat-infringer policy or acting promptly on notices (all in our Copyright page).

From the Copyright Office ([directory](https://www.copyright.gov/dmca-directory/), [FAQ](https://www.copyright.gov/dmca-directory/faq.html), primary):
- Fee USD 6 per designation, amendment or resubmission; a designation **expires after three years** unless renewed; the system emails reminders at 90, 60, 30 and 7 days. A lapse risks losing the safe harbour.
- One designation per **legal entity**, managed from one account; list app and website names (top-level domains) as **alternate names**; the agent may be a person, a role, a department or a third-party service. Only one agent per provider.
- Required: the provider's legal name and street address (a P.O. box only by prior approval), and the agent's name, organisation, mailing address, phone and email, plus an administrative contact. The information must also be shown on your website: use `company.dmca_agent` and `company.dmca_number` (shown on our Copyright page).
- No rule that the provider or agent must be in the US is stated on these pages (counsel should confirm for a Serbian company).
- Sign-in uses **Login.gov** (a US government service); you are prompted to create an account on first access, ideally with a business email. The Office pages do not say whether foreign users need identity verification, which MFA methods exist or what it costs: check [Login.gov help](https://login.gov/help/) when you create it.
- Apps that store no user content (or only the user's own private data, which is arguably still "stored at the direction of a user") are lower risk, but the registration is cheap; register if any app stores user uploads or posts.

## Other items found (not on your list)

1. **EU withdrawal function (Art. 11a Consumer Rights Directive, via Directive 2023/2673), from 19 June 2026**: a labelled withdrawal function plus confirmation for online distance contracts that carry a withdrawal right, not only financial services. National transposition and dates vary ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=66d0010e-40c0-47c4-8a10-7d503122cb8b), [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/risk-and-compliance/pitfalls-for-e-commerce-how-the-new-eu-withdrawal-button-widerrufsbutton-wi-102ms91), secondary). Implemented in the refunds text and the plugin (`WithdrawalLink`).
2. **COPPA written programs**: the amended rule adds retention and security duties and tighter parental consent; full compliance since 22 April 2026. Do not build features for under-13s without counsel.
3. **Email authentication**: SPF, DKIM and DMARC for bulk senders; keep spam complaints well under 0.3%.
4. **Magic-link hygiene**: single use, short life, stored hashed, POST confirm to survive mail scanners, no enumeration of known addresses.
5. **Other embeds that leak IP addresses**: YouTube, Maps, reCAPTCHA, chat widgets, CDNs, Gravatar, tag managers, as well as fonts.
6. **Uploads**: strip EXIF/GPS, serve from a separate origin with `nosniff`, never public by default.
7. **Dependency hygiene**: AI assistants invent package names ("slopsquatting"); verify every dependency and keep lockfiles. Production debug tools (Yii debug toolbar, Gii) off.
8. **Records**: consent records, renewal consents (3 years in California), suppression lists, DMCA renewals; backups; account deletion that really deletes.
9. **AI features**: EU AI Act transparency duties (Art. 50) from 2 August 2026 and chatbot disclosure laws in some US states (my own knowledge, **unverified** here).
10. **Accessibility**: the European Accessibility Act applies to many consumer services since 28 June 2025 (own knowledge, **unverified** here).
